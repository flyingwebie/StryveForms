Both passkeys and two-factor authentication (2FA) live in **Settings → Account**. Neither is required, but we recommend turning on 2FA — it stops someone from getting into your account even if they learn your password.

## Passkeys

A passkey replaces your password with your device's fingerprint reader, face unlock, or PIN. It's tied to a cryptographic key pair: the private half never leaves your device (or your password manager's encrypted sync), so a passkey can't be phished, guessed, or reused on another site the way a password can.

To add one, go to **Settings → Account → Passkeys → Add a passkey**, give it a name (e.g. "Work laptop"), and follow your browser or operating system's prompt. You can register as many as you like — one per device is typical. Rename or delete any of them from the same list; deleting one only removes that device's ability to sign in with it, your password and other passkeys keep working.

To sign in with a passkey, click **Sign in with a passkey** on the login page, or just start typing your email — supporting browsers offer to autofill a matching passkey.

## Two-factor authentication (2FA)

2FA adds a second step after your password: a 6-digit code from an authenticator app on your phone (Google Authenticator, 1Password, Authy, and similar apps all work — this uses the standard TOTP protocol, not a StryveForms-specific one).

### Turning it on

1. Go to **Settings → Account → Two-factor authentication → Turn on two-factor authentication**.
2. Confirm your current password.
3. Scan the QR code with your authenticator app, or enter the manual key shown underneath it if you can't scan.
4. Enter the 6-digit code your app now shows, to prove the setup worked.
5. Save your **backup codes** — copy or download them. Each one lets you sign in once without your authenticator app, for the day you lose your phone.

From then on, every sign-in with your password asks for a code too. Check **Trust this device for 30 days** on a device you use often, and it won't ask again there until that period is up.

If your admin configured an email fallback, you can click **Email me a code** on the verification step instead of using your app.

### Turning it off, or getting new backup codes

Both **Turn off** and **Regenerate backup codes** (Settings → Account → Two-factor authentication) ask for your current password first. Regenerating immediately invalidates your old backup codes — save the new ones before closing the dialog, they're shown exactly once.

### If you lose access

**You still have a backup code:** on the sign-in verification step, click **Use a backup code** and enter one instead of a 6-digit code. Each code works once; after using one, turn 2FA off and back on to get a fresh set, or regenerate backup codes once you're back in.

**You lost your authenticator app *and* your backup codes:** you can't get back in yourself — this is by design, 2FA wouldn't protect anything if it had a self-service bypass. Ask an instance admin to reset your two-factor authentication from **Admin → Users → Reset 2FA** next to your name. That clears your authenticator enrollment so you can sign in with just your password again; set 2FA up again afterwards with a new authenticator and backup codes.

Losing a passkey is never a lockout on its own — passkeys are an alternative to your password, not a requirement, so your password (or another passkey) still gets you in. Just delete the lost device's passkey from **Settings → Account → Passkeys** once you're signed in.

## Personal API tokens and MCP

A personal API token (**Settings → Developers**) and an MCP/OAuth bearer token authenticate a script, CLI, or AI agent directly — they don't go through the interactive sign-in form, so they're never challenged for 2FA and can't use a passkey. This is intentional: 2FA protects interactive sign-ins where a password could be phished or guessed; a token is a long-lived credential you already explicitly created and can revoke at any time from the same settings page.

## For instance admins: requiring 2FA

StryveForms doesn't yet enforce 2FA for instance admins — it's being considered as an opt-in **Admin → Access** setting ("Require two-factor authentication for instance admins", off by default). Until then, 2FA stays optional for everyone, including admins; we recommend admins turn it on themselves from Settings → Account.
